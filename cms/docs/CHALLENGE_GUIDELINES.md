# Challenge Creation Guidelines

## Core Principles

### 1. Identity-First Framing (Critical)

**NEVER:**
- "Good job completing the challenge!"
- "You're amazing!"
- "Keep up the great work!"

**ALWAYS:**
- "This is what [identity] does"
- "This is evidence you're someone who [trait]"
- "This is what [type of person] people do"

**Why:** Research shows "I am X" language creates lasting behavior change, while "I want X" or "Good job" creates dependency on external validation.

### 2. Specificity Over Vagueness

**Vague (Bad):**
- "Be more social today"
- "Exercise more"
- "Work on your goals"

**Specific (Good):**
- "Text someone you haven't talked to in a month"
- "Do 10 pushups before checking your phone"
- "Send one cold email to someone you admire in your field"

**Why:** Vague intentions fail. Specific actions succeed. The brain needs concrete steps, not abstract goals.

### 3. Implementation Intentions ("When X, Then Y")

Every challenge must include:

**Trigger:** "When [specific event/time/feeling]"
**Action:** "You will [specific action]"

Examples:
- "When you finish your morning coffee, open your messages and text that person"
- "When you wake up, do 10 pushups before touching your phone"
- "When you sit down at your desk, send that email you've been avoiding"

**Why:** Implementation intentions double or triple success rates (Gollwitzer & Sheeran research).

### 4. Meaningful Connection

Every challenge needs a "why" that connects the micro-action to macro-identity.

**Superficial (Bad):**
- "This will make you feel better"
- "You'll be glad you did it"

**Meaningful (Good):**
- "Every conversation you avoid creates distance. Every one you initiate builds trust"
- "Comfort is a drug. Discomfort is training. You're building your tolerance for hard things"
- "Skipping trains weakness. Showing up trains integrity with yourself"

**Why:** Small actions feel pointless without meaning. Connection to larger identity makes them matter.

### 5. Appropriate Difficulty Calibration

**LOW Difficulty:**
- 5-10 minutes
- Minimal social risk
- Physically easy
- Emotionally accessible
- Examples: "Text someone," "10 pushups," "Share one idea"

**MEDIUM Difficulty:**
- 10-15 minutes
- Moderate social risk
- Physically uncomfortable but not extreme
- Emotionally challenging but not traumatic
- Examples: "Have a difficult conversation," "2-minute cold shower," "Give critical feedback"

**HIGH Difficulty:**
- 15+ minutes
- High social risk (confrontation, vulnerability)
- Physically intense (ice bath, 100 burpees)
- Emotionally deep (shame, grief, trauma)
- Examples: "Apologize without explaining," "5-minute ice bath," "Share something you're ashamed of"

### 6. Evidence Type Selection

**PHOTO:** Physical challenges, visual proof needed (workout, meal, location)
**SCREENSHOT:** Digital actions (text conversations, social posts, emails sent)
**VOICE:** Reflections that benefit from verbal processing (emotional challenges)
**HONOR:** Internal experiences that can't be photographed (sitting with emotions, breathing exercises)

## Challenge Template

```markdown
Title: [Clear, action-oriented]

Description: [Specific instruction of exactly what to do, 2-3 sentences]

Zone: [social | physical | professional | emotional]

Difficulty: [low | medium | high]

Estimated Time: [5 | 10 | 15 minutes]

Implementation Trigger: "When you [specific event/time]"

Implementation Action: "You will [specific action]"

Identity Frame: "This is what [identity trait] people do"

Meaning Connection: "[Why this matters for their growth]"

Evidence Type: [photo | screenshot | voice | honor]
```

## Examples by Zone

### Social Zone Examples

**LOW:**
- Title: "Text Someone You Miss"
- Trigger: "When you finish reading this"
- Action: "Open messages and text that person"
- Identity: "This is what people who value relationships do"
- Meaning: "Every avoided conversation creates distance. Every initiated one builds trust"

**MEDIUM:**
- Title: "Set a Boundary"
- Trigger: "When someone asks for something you don't want to do"
- Action: "Say 'I can't do that' without over-explaining"
- Identity: "This is what people with healthy boundaries do"
- Meaning: "Every resentful yes trains self-abandonment. Every clear no trains self-respect"

**HIGH:**
- Title: "Tell Someone How They Hurt You"
- Trigger: "When you're with the person who hurt you"
- Action: "Say 'When you did X, I felt Y' without attacking"
- Identity: "This is what emotionally mature people do"
- Meaning: "Unexpressed hurt turns to resentment. Expressed hurt gives relationships a chance to heal"

### Physical Zone Examples

**LOW:**
- Title: "10 Pushups First Thing"
- Trigger: "When you wake up"
- Action: "Do 10 pushups before touching your phone"
- Identity: "This is what people who own their mornings do"
- Meaning: "How you start sets the tone. You're choosing discipline over drift"

**MEDIUM:**
- Title: "2-Minute Cold Shower"
- Trigger: "When you shower today"
- Action: "Take 2 minutes of fully cold water"
- Identity: "This is what resilient people do"
- Meaning: "Cold is sensation. Panic is optional. You're training your nervous system"

**HIGH:**
- Title: "5-Minute Ice Bath"
- Trigger: "When you've prepared your ice bath"
- Action: "Submerge for 5 full minutes"
- Identity: "This is what people who embrace suffering do"
- Meaning: "Extreme discomfort reveals who you are. You're meeting yourself at your edge"

### Professional Zone Examples

**LOW:**
- Title: "Share One Idea"
- Trigger: "In your next meeting"
- Action: "Share the idea you've been sitting on"
- Identity: "This is what contributors do"
- Meaning: "Unshared ideas die in silence. Shared ideas create value—even if rejected"

**MEDIUM:**
- Title: "Ask for a Raise Conversation"
- Trigger: "When you schedule with your manager"
- Action: "Request a conversation about compensation"
- Identity: "This is what people who advocate for themselves do"
- Meaning: "Silence about your value trains others to undervalue you"

**HIGH:**
- Title: "Quit Something Not Serving You"
- Trigger: "When you acknowledge what's draining you"
- Action: "Give notice or step back from that commitment"
- Identity: "This is what people who protect their growth do"
- Meaning: "Staying where you've outgrown is loyalty to stagnation, not commitment"

### Emotional Zone Examples

**LOW:**
- Title: "Name Your Current Feeling"
- Trigger: "Right now"
- Action: "Say out loud: 'I feel [emotion] about [thing]'"
- Identity: "This is what self-aware people do"
- Meaning: "Naming emotions reduces their power. Avoiding them increases it"

**MEDIUM:**
- Title: "Sit with Difficult Emotion"
- Trigger: "When a difficult emotion arises"
- Action: "Set a timer for 5 minutes and feel it fully"
- Identity: "This is what emotionally mature people do"
- Meaning: "Emotions aren't problems to solve. They're information to feel"

**HIGH:**
- Title: "Share What You're Ashamed Of"
- Trigger: "When you're with someone you deeply trust"
- Action: "Tell them something you're genuinely ashamed of"
- Identity: "This is what healing people do"
- Meaning: "Shame grows in silence and dies in connection. You're choosing freedom"

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Outcome-Based Instead of Identity-Based

**Wrong:** "Complete this challenge to build confidence!"
**Right:** "This is what confident people do"

### ❌ Too Vague

**Wrong:** "Be more productive today"
**Right:** "Work for 25 minutes without checking your phone"

### ❌ Missing Implementation Intention

**Wrong:** Just listing the action
**Right:** Always include "When X, you will Y"

### ❌ Generic Meaning

**Wrong:** "This is good for you"
**Right:** Specific connection to growth

### ❌ Wrong Difficulty Level

**Wrong:** "Have a difficult conversation" as LOW
**Right:** That's MEDIUM or HIGH depending on context

## Quality Checklist

Before submitting a challenge, verify:

- [ ] Title is clear and action-oriented
- [ ] Description is specific (not vague)
- [ ] Implementation intention includes trigger + action
- [ ] Identity frame starts with "This is what [identity]..."
- [ ] Meaning connection articulates WHY this matters
- [ ] Difficulty matches intensity (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH)
- [ ] Evidence type is appropriate
- [ ] Estimated time is realistic
- [ ] No outcome-based language ("to achieve X")
- [ ] Uses identity-first framing throughout

## Research References

**Identity-Based Habits (James Clear):**
- "I am a runner" > "I want to run"
- Focus on WHO you're becoming, not WHAT you're achieving

**Implementation Intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran):**
- 2-3x increase in success rates
- "When X, then Y" format

**Progress Principle (Teresa Amabile):**
- Small wins > big wins
- Frequency matters more than magnitude
- Progress must feel visible

**Tiny Habits (BJ Fogg):**
- Start absurdly small
- Anchor to existing behaviors
- Celebrate immediately

**Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan):**
- Autonomy: User chooses when to act
- Competence: Challenge is achievable
- Relatedness: Framed in identity community

---

**Remember:** Every challenge is a micro-intervention in someone's identity. Take it seriously. Make it specific. Connect it to meaning. Frame it in who they're becoming.
